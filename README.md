# Coding Agents 2.0

Coding Agents represent a vast economic opportunity that is being squandered due to bad ergonomics. We should get agency from agents.

The body of knowledge called Human Factors is vital to understanding productivity with automation. AI companies ignore this because they don't want humans to be a factor.

It is entirely predictable that a movement willfully disregarding who technology is meant to serve will produce bottlenecks and frustration instead of the miracles being promised by wholesale labor automation.

We are building a coalition supporting balanced approaches and will share our directions on solutions.

## We believe

* Contributors add value by being cognitively engaged.
* Work must be teachable, not ineffable "taste".
* Teams succeed together.
* Production codebases must stay maintainable.
* Usable abstractions are intentionally designed.
* Tools are for people to use, not compete with.
* Consent matters (right of refusal).

By examining the assumptions of [Coding Agents 1.0](./v1-explained.md) in light of these principles, we will promote tools that better meet our needs.

## Evidence of failure

* [Artificial Intelligence in the Firm: Bottlenecks in Software Production](https://fion.ac/jellyfish.pdf), 2026 Harvard Study by Fiona Chen and James Stratton. It studied 300 million work events across 718 firms.
